feat: send side of PTT, from the button to the wire (Task25)

Press and speak, not press, wait, speak. Capture starts on the button and
the peer is allowed half a second to answer, so the frames captured while
waiting are encoded straight away and held; the moment VOICE_ACCEPT
arrives they go out in order, ahead of the live ones. The flush and the
switch to live sending happen inside one critical section, because a
frame that slipped between them would be transmitted ahead of older
buffered audio and the receiver would play the words out of order.

The pipeline lives in :core, not in :app. Everything it needs is already
there -- the packetizer, the codec interface, the config -- and the only
part that belongs to the platform is handing bytes to a socket, which is
now the one-method DatagramSink. So "does the audio go out in the order
it was captured" is a JVM assertion rather than something two phones in a
room might show. app.network.UdpDatagramSink is the whole adapter.

SessionSignals.voiceEnd loses its two count parameters. VOICE_END carries
the last frame's sequence number and the frame count, and only whatever
sent the frames knows them: the state machine counts no frames, and a
frame the codec refused or the socket dropped never became a sequence
number. Passing them from above could only ever be a guess, which is what
the two zeroes were.

A fresh codec per transmission. Codec state is carried between frames,
and the peer creates a new decoder for every session it accepts, so an
encoder reused from the last transmission predicts against history the
far end has never seen -- and the frames that decode wrong are the first
ones, which are exactly the ones the pre-roll buffer exists to protect.

One collector, not four subscriptions. The send pipeline needs to know
when the peer accepted, the power locks exist only while a session does,
the foreground service type carries microphone only while transmitting,
and the UI needs to see the state. There are seven ways a session ends --
the button coming up, BUSY, no answer, a force interrupt, WiFi going
away, a phone call taking the audio focus, the service stopping -- and
four subscriptions would be four chances to handle one of those paths in
three of them. What that leaves behind is a wake lock, or a microphone
indicator that never goes out. VoiceSessionCoordinator is the collector,
and it is the last lifecycle step, so it is released first: a session has
to be ended and its last packet actually sent while the sockets are open.

The platform rule is checked before the machine is told anything.
Android 14 will not promote a foreground service to the microphone type
from the background, so transmitting requires a visible screen while
receiving does not. PttController asks AppVisibility, then promotes, then
calls requestTalk; a refusal costs nothing and the microphone is never
opened by a request that was going to fail. The promotion can still be
refused -- the app can be backgrounded between the question and the
answer -- and both exceptions the platform throws become
SendFailure.APP_NOT_VISIBLE rather than an exception out of a button
press. Dropping back to connectedDevice is driven by the session state,
not by the button.

Power locks per ADR-005 section 6: WIFI_MODE_FULL_LOW_LATENCY and a
PARTIAL_WAKE_LOCK with the session's own five-minute maximum as its
timeout. The timeout is the last line of defence rather than a nicety --
if a process stops running its own release path, the only thing that will
notice is the platform.

Recording is a seam, not a stub. VoiceRecording has the four moments a
file needs, including discard, which is the half of "BUSY and a timeout
produce no history record" that has to happen in the pipeline. Task38
implements it; until then it is NoVoiceRecording.

Also wired: the session machine into the transport, the validator's view
of the receiving session, presence taking its busy flag straight from the
machine rather than a mirrored copy, network loss ending the session as
INTERRUPTED instead of letting it trail off, and audio focus loss doing
the same. The debug screen gets a hold-to-talk button per peer, which is
the only way this task can be accepted on two devices; it goes with the
placeholder in Task32.

Twenty-five new tests in :core: the pre-roll ring's ordering and its
drop-the-oldest rule, and the pipeline's ordering, sequence numbering,
VOICE_END counts, refusal handling, codec lifetime and recording
lifecycle. The pipeline tests also assert that no SessionSignals method
actually suspends, because every one of them is called while the mutex
that arbitrates session ownership is held.

Known gap: the receiver still has no audio path. B accepts a session and
opens its speaker, but decoding and the jitter buffer are Task26, so
nothing is heard yet.
